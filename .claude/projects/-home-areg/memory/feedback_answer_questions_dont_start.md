When areg asks a question — including a question *about* a task ("which model
is better for this?", "how would you approach X?") — answer it and stop. Do
not begin the work. He says "go" / "go ahead" explicitly when he wants
execution to start.

**Why:** In the system-cleanup session he opened with a task description
followed by "is opus better for this? or fable?" I answered and immediately ran
the first audit batch. His reply: "i never told you to start...." After he
clarified scope a few turns later, I read it as a green light and started
again: "i didnt say go damn it..." Twice in one session, both times because a
question about the work looked like a mandate for it.

**How to apply:** Treat a question as a question. Describing a task, scoping
it, ruling something out of it, or asking which approach is better are all
still pre-authorization. Answer, then offer the next step in one line and wait.
His "go" is unambiguous when it comes — no need to infer it from enthusiasm or
momentum.

This is the mirror of [[feedback_execute_after_answers]], not a contradiction:
once *he* has answered *your* clarifying questions, execute without re-asking.
The distinction is who asked. His answers authorize; his questions do not.
