# Rate request from my frontend developer — facts

I'm the founder/CEO of a small crypto trading platform (Altrady, Netherlands,
roughly 10–20 people). I'm a software engineer myself and still write a lot of
the code. My long-time freelance frontend developer has asked to renegotiate
his rate and hours, and I want an independent view before I answer.

## The developer

- Freelance frontend engineer, working for us since **September 2018**
  (~8 years, continuous). Armenian, but grew up and was educated in the
  Netherlands — fluent Dutch, Dutch work culture; lives mostly in Armenia and
  the Philippines. Fully remote, different timezone, flexible hours; takes
  vacation as scattered days.
- Hourly contract, invoiced in 160-hour blocks. No equity, no benefits.
- He invoices as a private individual from abroad, no VAT (reverse charged),
  no tax ID. For me it's a simple foreign invoice: the hourly rate is my full
  cost — no employer contributions, no VAT cash-flow, no Dutch payroll or
  false-self-employment (DBA) exposure, no notice period.
- Has always been reliable and low-maintenance; no performance issues.
- Main product: an Electron/React desktop trading app (~1M lines touched over
  the years). He is the author of about **51 % of all commits** in that repo
  (≈5,700 of ≈11,200, 2018–2026) and, until this year, the primary frontend
  developer. Yearly share of commits: 2018 73 %, 2019 72 %, 2020 49 %,
  2021 55 %, 2022 35 %, 2023 60 %, 2024 52 %, 2025 74 %, 2026 so far ≈ 20 %.
- Also contributes to our exchange-API client library, mobile webview, and
  this year to a new charting engine ("Superchart", developed with an external
  developer) that replaces our old TradingView integration. He is leading the
  integration of that engine into the desktop app — started early 2026,
  planned to ship around November 2026. It is the largest frontend change of
  the year.

## What changed in 2026

- Since early this year we do most of our coding with an LLM assistant
  (Claude). My own output and our UI designer's output went up a lot:
  commits this year so far — designer ≈ 770, me ≈ 540, the developer ≈ 290
  (+~60 in the Superchart repo). Last year it was developer 610, me 180,
  designer 40.
- The developer's work has shifted toward specification, architecture and
  review; he writes "more English than code" and is clearly more productive per
  hour than before.
- The designer now contributes application code with LLM help. There is no
  formal senior review step for frontend contributions; the developer does
  informal review when he sees things.
- We have not hired additional developers.
- It is possible that total frontend demand goes down from here: features ship
  faster with LLM help, the backlog of big new features is shorter than it used
  to be, and the designer and I absorb more routine frontend work ourselves.

## Business context

- Revenue is subscription-based plus exchange affiliate income; it moves with
  the crypto market cycle. I'd rather not disclose numbers. Observable facts:
  we have paid him on time every month since 2018, paid a bonus every year
  including through the 2022–23 bear market, have not laid anyone off, and in
  2026 hired an external developer to build the new charting engine. Team size
  has been stable for years; we have not hired additional in-house developers.
  Assume a small SaaS that is profitable but not rich, and answer for both a
  good year and a tight year.
- Replacing him would mean hiring another senior remote React developer and
  onboarding them into an 8-year-old codebase they have no history with, while
  the Superchart integration is in flight.
